# Final year masters thesis 2025-2026 #
Title - Rules versus Enforcement: Stablecoin Peg Stability Around MiCA and Binance's Delisting Announcement.

# Abstract #
Stablecoins have become an important part of digital-asset markets, with their market capitalization 
increasing sharply since 2020 from USD 5 billion to USD 323 billion as of May 2026. Their usefulness 
depends on their ability to remain close to their intended peg which is usually 1 USD. Deviations 
from the one-dollar peg can indicate pressure on market confidence and liquidity and the ability of 
stablecoins to function as trading pairs, settlement instruments and liquidity bridges. This makes the 
regulation of stablecoins important not only as a legal issue but also a potential source of market 
instability. The European Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation introduced a new regulatory 
distinction between compliant and non-compliant stablecoins, and major exchanges were required 
to adjust the availability of affected assets for European Economic Area users. This thesis examines 
whether MiCA-related regulatory developments affected the short-run peg stability of USD-pegged 
stablecoins on Binance. The analysis focuses on Binance hourly price data and compares stablecoins 
affected by MiCA-related restrictions with USDC, which serves as the MiCA-compliant control 
stablecoin. Treated stablecoins include FDUSD, TUSD, and USDP. Absolute peg deviation is calculated 
as the absolute difference between each stablecoin’s hourly price and its intended one-dollar peg. I 
estimate difference-in-differences models around two events: the broad MiCA stablecoin-rule 
application date on 30 June 2024 and Binance’s exchange-specific restriction announcement on 3 
March 2025. The results show that the broad MiCA application date does not produce a statistically 
significant increase in peg deviations. In contrast, Binance’s 3 March 2025 announcement is 
associated with a positive and statistically significant increase in peg deviations for treated 
stablecoins relative to USDC. The estimated effect is approximately 4.34 basis points. Robustness 
checks show that the effect is similar for both high-volume and low-volume treated stablecoins. 
Overall, the findings suggest that exchange-specific MiCA-related restrictions had a stronger shortrun effect on stablecoin peg stability than the broad regulatory application date itself
